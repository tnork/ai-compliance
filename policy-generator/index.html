Does the firm have a WISP (Pub 5708)?WISP stands for Written Information Security Plan.It’s a written document that federal law requires every professional tax preparer to have. The FTC Safeguards Rule (under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act) treats tax preparers as “financial institutions,” so anyone with a PTIN must maintain a WISP describing how they protect client data - access controls, encryption, incident response, employee training, and so on. The IRS confirms this on the annual PTIN renewal.Failure to comply with the Safeguards Rule can carry FTC penalties of roughly $53,088 per violation.A firm’s WISP has to account for where client data flows. AI tools are a new data flow, so supervised, documented AI use (approved tools, PII rules, personal-account boundaries) is what keeps the WISP honest - which is why this policy’s PII-handling and shadow-AI sections map to it.WISP obligations are about data security (§7216/§6713 disclosure penalties, FTC enforcement), a separate legal track from Circular 230’s supervision duties (§§10.22/10.36) - AI Compliance’s record covers evidence for both.
Does the firm have an Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)?AUP stands for Acceptable Use Policy.It’s a written document that sets the rules for how your staff may use the firm’s technology - computers, email, networks, the internet, and increasingly AI tools. It usually spells out what’s allowed, what’s prohibited, and each person’s responsibility for protecting firm and client information.It’s different from a WISP: a WISP is a data-security plan federal law requires every tax preparer to have, while an Acceptable Use Policy is a broader governance document many firms choose to keep. An AI-use policy often works alongside both.In AI Compliance you can upload your Acceptable Use Policy (and other firm documents) under Policies, keep a version history, and have staff acknowledge each version. It’s optional - not required to use AI Compliance.AI Compliance doesn’t write your AUP for you or judge whether a given policy is adequate. Your policies and their legal sufficiency remain your firm’s and your counsel’s responsibility.
